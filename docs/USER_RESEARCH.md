Observation 1 - Mason DePalma:
- Observation Statement: I observe that a majority of people really wanted to see a dashboard that would summarize both what they are owed and also what they owe. 
- Supporting Data: 68% percent of responses chose this option in the feature list. 
- Team action: We will focus on planning out this feature in an early sprint. 

Observation 2 - Bryan Rich
- Observation Statement: All of the people to take the survey are college students 
- Supporting Data: In the question “Which of the following best describes you?” 100% of respondents selected college student.
- Team Action : As a team, we will focus on making our target audience the younger generation, especially college students. We will design our app to be simple and easy to use, with features that fit the needs of students who frequently borrow money, items, or favors from friends. 

Observation 3 - Jesse Hopkins:
- Observation Statement: Many respondents reported that they rarely exchange money or items with friends, suggesting that the IOU app should prioritize simplicity and convenience.
- Supporting Data: 10 out of 19 respondents said they exchange money or items less than once a month or never.
- Team Action: This suggests users would benefit from an app that allows them to quickly record and track IOUs without unnecessary features or complicated navigation.

Observation 4 - Ryan Johnson:
- Observation Statement: The feature most sought after is based around tracking money owed between friends rather than favors or lent items.
- Supporting Data: 68.4% of responses choose the monetary option as a feature they would want to see on the app compared to 47.4% for lent items and favors a lower percentage.
- Team Action: Our primary focus will be on the monetary items between friends and users as opposed to favors and items with that being the first problem area we will tackle.
